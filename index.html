Pilot

The applicant's side of document upload. Each person gets a list built from their own application, asking only for what applies to them and offering the shortest path the rules allow, like one benefits letter instead of everyone's pay stubs. Anything the agent asks for beyond the rules is labeled.

One sample household picked in a lottery. Everything here is fictional: the household, the building and the documents.

Try these

  1. No benefits path. Answer "No" to the benefits question, then add one of Maria's pay stubs. You'll see the two-stub rule.
  2. Employer mismatch. Add Luis's Fresh Market stub. His application says Metro Deli.
  3. Benefits path. Restart, answer "Yes," and try each sample letter.
  4. Each adult's own list. Send Luis his link, then switch to his view.
  5. Languages. Switch between English, Spanish, Simplified Chinese, Traditional Chinese and Russian at the top of the app.
  6. Agent's view. Use "Jump to the agent's view" below, or add #agent to the link. If you haven't started, it shows a sample household that's partly done.
  7. Reminders. Leave a few items missing, then preview the texts each adult would get 2 weekdays before the deadline.

What it shows

  • A checklist built from the application, grouped by person
  • Checks that warn and explain, and never block
  • Forms signed in the same flow
  • A countdown to the deadline, and reminder texts
  • Every applicant screen in five languages

Not shown: the voucher path

The voucher shortcut (PRD R3) is left out on purpose. It reuses the pattern the benefits letter already shows: one document replaces income items. It applies only in non-tax-credit units (Handbook 4-5.B.4), so it's a building setting, not a new screen. The prototype spends its time where households fall out at upload: pay stubs and mismatches, behind the 399 households dropped for missing documents in NYHC's case study. Voucher holders' bigger problem there comes before upload: they're contacted for units their voucher doesn't cover.

Tap "Why?" on any item to see the rule behind it. Sources: HPD/HDC Marketing Handbook (April 2025), cited by section; Attachment H-1C (April 1, 2025).

Translations are samples written for this prototype; a launch would use professional translation. This prototype doesn't remember the language you pick; a live app would save each person's choice so it's the default every time they return. The agent's view stays in English.
